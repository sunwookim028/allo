4. D-23: the command boundary, and the deadlock/issue-rate check
================================================================

**The interface.** ``vpu_ctrl_t`` is three slot commands, each a ``Stream``
carrying one token per valid command in its issue cycle: V (5 valids + 42
payload bits), X (``vmem.valid`` + 19: op, register, resolved word, the
store hint), M (3 valids + 15). Gating is structural: no token, no payload --
D-23's allowed deviation, masked in the check (§2).

**The four resources** (``vpu_cmd.resources()``, ``logs/delta_and_probes.log``):
``vreg`` with ports ``ra``/``rb`` (r, latency 0) owned by the V unit, ``rc``
(r, 0) owned by an arbiter fed by the store's request and the matrix
stream's (the RTL's "the matrix read wins silently", ``vpu.sv:430``, as the
owner's declared priority), ``w`` owned by W1; ``vmem.c`` (rw, latency 3)
owned by the X unit. It **composes**, and builds and runs on the simulator
and csim. **Refused at composition, naming the port**: the X unit also
reading ``vreg.ra``; the store reading ``vreg.rc`` itself beside the arbiter;
the load unit writing ``vreg.w`` beside W1 (and H7's second writer in
``vpu_wb``). Dropping the arbiter is refused first by the netlist rule on its
dangling channel.

**Deadlock.** The netlist is acyclic (issue -> three Streams -> receivers;
the resources composition likewise), so no ``deadlock_free_because`` is
owed; each command channel has one writer and one reader (the netlist
rules). What remains is the rate.

**Issue rate, measured in csim** (``scripts/d23_rate.py``,
``logs/d23_rate_csim.log``). The emitted ``kernel.cpp`` is stamped (the
``u1_pipe`` technique: a patch to a generated file, values still held to the
reference): the cycle at the top of each issue-loop iteration, after each
command ``Push``, after each receiver ``Pop``. Loop iteration ``t`` is RTL
cycle ``t``.

     - intervals of 1

**Result: in csim the three Streams cost neither a deadlock nor an issue
cycle at depth >= 2; the sequencer issues cycle for cycle as the RTL does on
every Phase 0 program.** At depth 1 a stall the RTL does not have appears
(488 cycles on back-to-back commands): a finding (F-B3), and the obligation's
"sized" is a legality, depth >= 2, not a free choice.

**On Catapult RTL: not measured here; left for track D**, with two reasons to
expect the depth to matter more there: ``u3_fifo_composed`` measured a
producer stall (238 cycles, 0.6 %) on Catapult RTL through a 4-deep
Connections FIFO where csim showed none, and ``minitpu_rtl_mxu`` measured a
depth-2 link between two II-1 kernels sustaining 2 tokens per 3 cycles. A
csyn of ``vpu_cmd:streams`` was started at 3.33 ns (§7). Track D's check:
``d23_rate.py``'s three numbers on the Catapult RTL in Verilator, at depths 2
and 4. The **non-blocking fallback**, if needed, is the ``empty()``-guarded
form, not ``try_get``/``try_put``: the emitter lowers ``empty()`` to the
FIFO's ``empty_o`` and a guarded ``Pop`` with one ``wait()`` an iteration
(seen in ``vpu_wb:selftimed``'s ``kernel.cpp``), whereas limitation 18 (issue
#23, open) turns ``try_*`` into blocking calls.

7. Catapult
===========

**Not built to RTL here.** One csyn of ``vpu_cmd:streams`` (n 64, 3.33 ns,
every iteration loop pipelined, inner loops unrolled -- which unrolls the
64-iteration end-of-trace pad loop with its three conditional puts; track C's
``u3c_build.py`` unchanged) passed ``go analyze``/``compile`` and spent 13 min
in ``architect``, reaching **86 GB** of memory, and was stopped by hand on a
shared host (no result, no manifest). Likely cause, untested: the unrolled
pad loop (64 x 3 conditional ``Push``\ es) -- a harness artifact, not the
issue logic; a Catapult build should drop the pad loop (the receivers' trip
count is a harness need) or keep it rolled. The cycle-for-cycle issue check
on Catapult RTL and every area number are **track D's** (D-23's condition on
Catapult RTL is open). F-B10 in §6.

10. Follow-up: ``result_latency.vmatpush`` at the issue edge; deltas re-keyed to ``v1``
=======================================================================================

minitpu-comp confirmed ``b3ba0a4d`` is ``v1`` (F-B1 resolved: ``v1-course`` is
``613190d``'s timing; ``05e1bdf``, an ancestor of the pin, brought the one-beat
``vmatpop`` and 82 -> 85) and asked to reconcile their 85 with U3's MXU
push->valid 82.

**Definition.** Issue to issue. ``asm.py``: ``_M_RESULT_LATENCY = 85``, "Cycles
from a vmatpush to its first result; each vmatpop waits on the OLDEST
un-popped push", used as ``ready = max(ready, cycle_of[push] + 85)`` for the
pop's issue cycle; ``docs/isa_latency.json``: "cycles from vmatpush to the
first vmatpop that finds its result waiting"; ``docs/ISA_AND_INTERFACES.md``:
"a vmatpop issues no earlier than 85 cycles after the oldest vmatpush not yet
popped". U3's 82 is at the MXU port: last ``input_push`` to ``output_valid``.

**Measured** (``scripts/mxu_offset.py``, ``logs/mxu_offset.log``): asm.py
programs through the sequencer RTL (Phase 0's ``Run``), its ``vpu_ctrl_o``
into ``vpu.sv`` row for row (``minitpu_core``'s wiring: no register between),
the MXU port read by hierarchical reference (``units/rtl/u4_vpu_mxu.sv``).
From the vmatpush's issue row: rows at the MXU at **+1..+4**, ``output_valid``
at **+86** (82 after the last push); ``asm.schedule()`` puts the vmatpop at
**+85**, which reaches ``output_pop`` at +86 and the writeback mux at +86 =
pop + 1, as the calendar books it. A pop forced at +82/+83/+84 is taken at
+86 all the same, 3/2/1 cycles later than booked, with no assertion; at +86
or later it is taken the next cycle. So **85 = 82 + 4 - 1**: the last of the
four rows, then the pop engine's register. The issue-edge offset is **+3**
(not "+ the issue-to-port offset" alone: the pop's own register takes one back).

**Re-keyed deltas** (``template/gen_isa_delta``): every block relative to base
``v1``, each ``source`` naming the Allo commit and this record.
``allo-locked`` 8/8 DELTA-MATCH ``v1``. ``allo-mxu`` (``--mxu-measured
minitpu_rtl_mxu_2026-10-08/logs/measured_dim2q4.json``): result latency 20 + 3
= **23**, vmatpop interval **9**, ``WB_W_MPOP_LAST`` 3, output FIFO 64; the same
function on ``b3ba0a4d``'s own port numbers (82, 1) reproduces ``v1``'s 85, 1,
3, 64 (``logs/mxu_base_check.log``). Does not reconcile: the Allo MXU was
measured at **DIM 2** while the profile is 16 lanes (the MXU record's
generator kept these ``unresolved`` for that reason; they are emitted as
overrides on request, with the DIM in ``source``); the pop interval 9 is a
port measurement assumed issue-to-issue (both pops carry the same register),
not measured through the sequencer.
