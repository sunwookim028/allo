``acc24 SystemC`` is ``native_bitext``. Outside NaN, the simulator's
``native`` is IEEE bit for bit: ``bf16_mul`` 6,019,104/6,019,104 against
``ref.ieee_bf16_mul``; ``mul_acc24`` 6,019,073/6,019,104 against

backends: ``ac_std_float`` has gradual underflow too.

What the results say
====================

* **Both multipliers are expressible bit-exactly in Allo on both backends**,
  as integer-field transcriptions of the ``.sv``. The comb ``vpu_bf16_mul``
  needed no workaround at all: it computes the exponent as ``logic signed
  [10:0]``, so its compares are signed and B1 never applies. ``mul_acc24``
  compares only against literals, which B1 does not touch either.
* **Allo's float types cannot state either multiplier.** ``bfloat16`` has IEEE
  gradual underflow and no flush-to-zero mode; there is no acc24 type at all.
  1.0 M (mul) and 1.35 M (acc24) of 6.0 M vectors differ, all by the RTL's
  named rules (semantic mismatch, as for ``bf16_add``; the owner's triage
  item 3 now has a flush-to-zero rule as well as NaN/zero rules).
* **The pipelined unit is the comb unit, to Allo.** See L1-L3.

Findings
========

Classes are README D-9's. B1, M1-M5 and S1-S3 are in
``u1_bf16_add_bits_2026-10-02.rst``; only new ones, or new evidence, here.
Repros: ``dev/records/minitpu/u1_mul/repros.py {s4,s5,n1}`` and
``dev/records/minitpu/u1_mul/latency_probe.py``.

B1 (again). The pipe's 9-bit unsigned exponent compare -- **bug**, new site
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

``vpu_bf16_mul_pipe`` stage 2 says ``exp_sum_s2 <= exp_bias_s2`` on two
``logic [8:0]``. Transcribed as ``UInt(9) <= UInt(9)`` it is a signed
``cmpi sle``, so every ``exp_sum_s2 >= 256`` (both exponents large) reads as
negative and the product is flushed to zero: **1,229,630 / 6,019,104** wrong
(``3f80*7f00: allo 0000 rtl 7f00``). Workaround: compare 10-bit copies
(``sum10``/``bias10``), marked ``(B1)`` in ``bits_pipe`` and ``stages``. The
other compare at that site, ``exp_sum_s2 >= exp_bias_s2 + 255``, is safe:
the literal widens the right side to a signed ``Int(33)`` (M4's bottom-up
widths, here helping). Same function in the comb module, no B1: the two RTL
forms of one unit differ in whether Allo computes them right.

N1. NaN sign: simulator and SystemC disagree on one Allo program -- **semantic mismatch** (between backends)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

``c = a * b`` on ``bfloat16``: the simulator (LLVM on x86) gives a NaN the
sign of the NaN operand, and ``Inf x 0`` the x86 default NaN ``0xFFC0``;
SystemC's ``ac::bfloat16`` gives every NaN the sign ``a ^ b``. Over the
1,067,862 distinct stimulus pairs with a NaN or Inf operand the two backends
differ on **403,397**. In float32 (``mul_acc24`` native) the simulator also
keeps the operand's payload (``7f81 -> 7fc100``), ``ac_ieee_float<binary32>``
does not (272,888 more differences in that class). MiniTPU gives ``+0x7FC0``
always. For ``bf16_add`` the pilot saw a third encoding (``ac_std_float``
``0x7FFF``) -- so the NaN a bf16 op returns depends on the operation and the
backend, and the simulator is no stand-in for csim on NaNs.

.. code-block:: text

   a*b for a=[7fc0 ffc0 7f80 0000], b=[bf80 3f80 0000 ff80]
   simulator  7fc0 ffc0 ffc0 ffc0
   systemc    ffc0 ffc0 7fc0 ffc0
   MiniTPU    7fc0 7fc0 7fc0 7fc0

N2. No flush-to-zero float -- **missing abstraction** (semantic mismatch with the RTL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both multipliers flush subnormal operands and products below ``2^-126``; Allo's
``bfloat16`` and ``float32`` are IEEE with gradual underflow in both backends,
with no attribute or mode to change that. 603,316 (mul) and 674,119 (acc24)
vectors, the four flush rows above. Only ``bits`` matches. Proposal: fold into
triage item 3(b), a float type's *policy* (NaN encoding, signed-zero rule,
denormal mode) as part of the type, since every machine has its own.

W1. acc24 through float32 and bitcasts -- **workaround** (missing abstraction: no custom float format)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

``native`` multiplies in ``float32``, bitcasts to ``uint32``, rounds to 15
fraction bits by adding ``0x7F + lsb`` and returns ``bits >> 8`` in a
``uint32``: the shape of ``microarch.py``'s ``acc24`` and
``test_acc24_emulation_via_bitcast``. It is exact where the product is a
float32 normal, but where it is a float32 subnormal the product is rounded
twice (float32, then acc24): **31** vectors differ from IEEE acc24 by one
acc24 ulp (``105b*a99f: allo 800008 ieee 800009``). All 31 are below
``2^-126``, which the RTL flushes, so the RTL comparison does not see them
(20 of them round to zero and so even agree with the RTL: the flush row has
88,417 here against IEEE's 88,437) -- the emulation is wrong only where this
RTL happens not to care. A
``Float(e=8, m=15)`` (or ``ac_std_float<24,8>``-like) type would remove it.

S4. SystemC emitter: ``bf16 -> f32`` widening does not compile -- **bug**
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

``av: float32 = a[i]`` with ``a: bfloat16[n]`` (an ``arith.extf``) is emitted
as copy-initialisation, ``ac_ieee_float<binary32> v4 = v3;`` with ``v3`` an
``ac::bfloat16``. The converting constructor is ``explicit``, so g++ rejects
it ("conversion from 'ac::bfloat16' to non-scalar type
'ac_ieee_float<binary32>' requested"). Every float-widening kernel with a bf16
input fails csim (``test_acc24_emulation_via_bitcast`` has the same widening;
it runs on the simulator only). Checked with Catapult's g++ 10.3 on a 6-line C++ file:
``ac_ieee_float<binary32> f(b);`` compiles and gives ``1.5`` for ``1.5``.
*Proposed fix:* ``VhlsModuleEmitter::emitCast``
(``mlir/lib/Translation/EmitVivadoHLS.cpp:2613``), which the Catapult and
SystemC emitters inherit, emits ``T v = x;``; for ``ExtFOp``/``TruncFOp``
between two ``ac`` float types the SystemC emitter should emit
``T v = T(x);``. ``TruncFOp`` (f32 -> bf16) is probably the same; not
checked. *Workaround* (variant ``native_bitext``): widen on bit patterns,
``ab: uint16 = a[i].bitcast(); aw: uint32 = ab; au: uint32 = aw << 16; av:
float32 = au.bitcast()``. That needs M4's explicit widening before the
shift.

S5. SystemC csim cannot read back a ``UInt(24)`` port -- **bug** (runner)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

``mul_acc24``'s natural port is ``UInt(24)[n]``. The simulator accepts it
with a numpy ``uint32`` array and the ``bits`` variant matches with it; SystemC
csim compiles and runs, then ``read_tensor_from_file`` (``allo/backend/vitis.py:496``)
raises ``KeyError: 'ui24'`` because ``allo.utils.np_supported_types`` has only
power-of-two widths. The committed variants use ``uint32`` ports (bits 31:24
zero), a mild distortion of the interface. *Proposed fix:* map ``ui<N>``/``i<N>``
to the smallest numpy container, as the simulator's argument path already does.

L1. No way to state a unit's latency -- **missing abstraction**
---------------------------------------------------------------

``vpu_bf16_mul_pipe`` is ``vpu_bf16_mul`` with two register banks (latency 2,
II 1). In Allo:

* the **simulator** is untimed: the comb and pipe forms are the same program
  and give the same 6,019,104 results; the harness's ``latency=2`` on the pipe
  rows is the RTL's, not checked on the Allo side;
* ``s.pipeline("mul_0:i")`` states an **initiation interval**, not a depth;
  the depth is the HLS tool's choice (Catapult gave ``bf16_add`` latency 2 at
  2.0 ns and 1 at 3.33 ns, ``u1_bf16_add_catapult_2026-10-02``). It does not
  change csim timing (L2);
* there is no primitive for "register here" or "this unit's latency is 2"
  (``allo/customize.py`` has ``pipeline(axis, initiation_interval, rewind,
  style)`` and nothing with latency). So a pipelined leaf can be written only
  as its function plus a schedule hint; the latency the rest of MiniTPU relies
  on (``vpu_alu`` aligns the multiplier with ``vpu_bf16_add_pipe``) is not in
  the Allo program.

For U1 the pipe is therefore **the comb unit's verdicts plus a recorded
deviation (latency not expressible)**, the same call as triage item 4 for
latency 0.

L2. A Stream split at the register boundary is not a register -- **semantic mismatch** (csim timing)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

``stages`` puts each ``*_s1_q`` register on its own ``Stream[T, 1]`` between
kernel ``s1`` (the DSP product) and kernel ``s2`` (normalize, round,
specials). Functionally it is exact on both backends. In time
(``latency_probe.py``: testbench push/pop stamped with ``sc_time_stamp()``,
1 ns clock, n = 8), measured as cycles from inputs accepted to output popped:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1

   * - Allo program
     - csim latency
     - csim II
     - RTL
   * - ``bf16_mul.bits``
     - 2
     - 1
     - comb: 0
   * - ``bf16_mul.bits`` + ``s.pipeline("mul_0:i")``
     - 2
     - 1
     -
   * - ``bf16_mul_pipe.bits_pipe``
     - 2
     - 1
     - pipe: 2, II 1
   * - ``stages`` (depth-1 Streams)
     - 4, then growing
     - **2**
     -
   * - ``stages(depth=2)``
     - 4
     - 1
     -

So csim's timing is a property of the threads' ``wait()``\ s and the
Connections buffers, not of the unit: comb and pipe both take 2 cycles, a
``s.pipeline`` changes nothing, and a stream hop adds 2 cycles. A depth-1
Stream, the obvious way to write "one register", **halves the throughput**: a
handshaked one-place buffer cannot accept while full, and a pipeline register
always can. Depth 2 restores II 1. Neither Allo backend can therefore check a
declared latency; only RTL that a tool wrote from the Allo program can (the
Catapult track).

L3. Nothing in Allo is a pipeline register -- **missing abstraction**
---------------------------------------------------------------------

``Stream`` is a FIFO (L2), ``Channel`` a handshake with no storage, ``Wire``
"zero storage and zero alignment" (``docs/source/backends/systemc.rst``,
limitation 22); ``Wire``/``Channel`` are SystemC-only (that page: every
other target raises ``NotImplementedError``; not re-tried here). None of them is "a register clocked every cycle,
no handshake", which is what ``vpu_bf16_mul_pipe``'s ``*_s1_q`` and the
MXU's PE registers are. *Proposal* (for the owner, with item 4): a
``Reg[T]``/``delay(x, k)`` link or a ``latency=`` on ``@df.kernel``/
``@df.unit`` that the simulator can ignore, the SystemC emitter can lower to
registered ``sc_signal``\ s, and an RTL comparison can hold the tool to.

What worked as written
======================

``UInt(8) * UInt(8)`` gives ``UInt(16)`` (no truncation of the DSP product);
``Int(11)`` locals carry the RTL's ``logic signed [10:0]`` exponent including
the ``- 126`` from an unsigned sum; four-term ``or`` conditions; slices of a
signed ``Int(11)`` (``exp_final[0:8]``); ``a[i].bitcast()`` on a ``bfloat16``
array element; a closure variable as a Stream depth (``Stream[T, depth]``);
a ``UInt(24)`` port in the simulator.
